# General synthesis and own-P&R joint objective

The latest user objective is a single general GW5AST-138 flow which improves UART, int2float, TMDS and VexRiscv individually by at least 20% against verified official synthesis plus official P&R. It supersedes the older VexRiscv-primary/regression-guard scheduling, without changing frozen historical evidence.

The full original implementation scope remains: native-derived device resources and timing, legal packing, timing-driven placement and legalization, congestion/timing-driven routing and physical optimization, independent connectivity/conflict/configuration checks, complete I/O STA, and a verifiable bitstream interface. Vendor final STA and encoding are allowed. Vendor synthesis, packing, placement and routing may be analyzed for fair baselines, native calibration and architecture research; vendor-generated physical results must never be represented as own P&R. No open chip database, automatic hardware programming, pin reassignment or interface-cycle changes.

Use the same algorithms and parameter-selection rules for all designs. Adaptation must follow graph/device features, not project names or special RTL edits. Jointly consider wide LUT mapping, registers/control sets, hard-resource boundaries and physical interconnect. Evaluate whole-design endpoint groups and spatial regions, not just a few worst paths.

Freeze fair official references before claiming ratios: identical RTL and defined-output semantics, device/speed grade, package pins, electrical constraints, I/O timing budget, clocks, interface cycles, corners and checks. Verify hold and other timing checks. Previously quoted vendor runs with hold failures, or different clock strategies, are historical evidence rather than accepted baselines. The primary score is the minimum of four Fmax ratios; success requires every ratio >=1.20 and all verification gates. Reference MHz are intentionally unset until audited.

The latest user edit replaces the fixed 110 MHz rule for future joint evaluations. Predeclare a uniform 2% headroom above each 1.20-times-baseline target: evaluation MHz = fair official baseline Fmax * 1.20 * 1.02. Both vendor and own candidates use this same frequency recognition and acceptance policy. After complete native STA with passing hold and all other required gates, directly recognize native reported Fmax without rerunning at that Fmax. Record actual constraint frequency, setup/hold margins and closure at that constraint separately. Establish the reference from a labelled scouting run, then audit its final STA at the derived evaluation constraint; if the final reported reference Fmax changes, the reference is not frozen until the derived contract is consistent. Never silently change headroom or relax I/O budgets. Historical 110 MHz results and independent-frequency replays retain their original policy.

Every complete optimization attempt including validation/export must finish within one hour. Never reset a running attempt deadline. Workspace must stay below 40,000,000,000 bytes, with worst-case output space reserved before writes. Consult Gemini proactively with open questions on alternative architectures and overlooked assumptions, and verify relevant recent primary research before adoption. User permits dependency-checked cleanup of superseded, non-frozen historical intermediates, with a manifest preserving source/configuration/hash provenance and success/failure conclusions. Delivered ZIP, frozen evidence, fair baselines and current implementation dependencies remain intact. Deleted intermediates must not be claimed as still directly verifiable.
